# Jordan

Jordan is a beautiful country in the Middle East known for its ancient ruins, desert landscapes, and warm hospitality. During our trip, we explored some of the country's most iconic sites — from the red-rose city of Petra to the vast Wadirum desert and the Roman ruins of Jerash.

- 👨‍👩‍👧‍👧 Trip type: Family  
- 🗓️ Duration: 7 days (2023-10-21 to 2023-10-27)  
- 📍 Highlights: Petra, Wadirum, Jerash

Petra, often referred to as the "Rose City" due to the color of the stone, is Jordan’s most famous archaeological site. It was established as early as the 4th century BCE by the Nabateans, an Arab people who developed it into a major trading hub. The city is renowned for its rock-cut architecture and advanced water conduit systems that allowed it to thrive in the arid desert environment.

We entered through the Siq, a narrow gorge that winds its way between towering cliffs, gradually revealing the iconic façade of the Treasury (Al-Khazneh). It felt like stepping into a hidden city, with the stone walls glowing orange and red in the morning light. Beyond the Treasury, Petra unfolds into a complex network of tombs, temples, and colonnaded streets. We hiked up to the Monastery (Ad Deir), a massive carved structure set high in the hills, and paused to look out over the rugged landscape that surrounds the ancient city. Even with the crowds, there was something surreal about walking through such an immense place carved entirely from stone.

Wadirum, also known as the "Valley of the Moon," is a protected desert wilderness in southern Jordan. It's characterized by vast red sand dunes and massive granite and sandstone rock formations. The area has been inhabited for thousands of years, and petroglyphs and inscriptions can still be found on the rocks, testifying to its long history as a crossroads for caravans and nomadic peoples. It's also famous as the setting for films like *Lawrence of Arabia*, *The Martian*, and *Dune*.

We spent a morning riding camels through the wide open desert, our shadows stretching long across the sand. Sometimes we stopped to admire the deep ochre canyons. As the sun set, the landscape turned golden. We sat under the stars, ate zarb (meat and vegetables cooked underground). Sleeping in tents surrounded by complete silence and a sky full of stars was an experience none of us will forget. Wadirum may be empty on a map, but on the ground, it is full of presence.

Jerash is one of the best-preserved Roman provincial towns in the world. Located in northern Jordan, the city was known as *Gerasa* in antiquity and flourished during the first few centuries CE. Its ruins today form a sprawling open-air museum of colonnaded streets, theaters, baths, temples, and plazas. One of its most striking features is the Oval Plaza — a large, unusually shaped public space bordered by a graceful colonnade.

Walking through Jerash, we imagined what life might have been like in Roman times. The amphitheater still has impressive acoustics; even a whisper carries from the center of the stage. We stood atop the Temple of Artemis, dedicated to the city’s patron goddess, and looked out over the site’s expansive layout. Roman chariot races were once held in the hippodrome, and parts of the city are still being uncovered by archaeologists. While Petra feels mythical and Wadirum feels wild, Jerash feels structured — a place of governance
